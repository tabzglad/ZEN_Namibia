# Namibia energy model data

This folder holds the raw data and assumptions for the Namibia energy model for 2023.

## Input files

The folders follow ZEN-europe: `<category>/<source>/`, with one source per
file. Each source has its own dataset class in `zen_namibia/datasets/datasets/`;
dataset collections combine sources. ZEN Namibia's own modelling assumptions
are all in one file, `05-assumptions/zen_namibia_assumptions.csv`.

## How the 2023 model is set up

The demand profile has 8,760 hourly values. The two missing year-end values were filled, then the profile was scaled to NamPower's 2023 figures: 4,055 GWh of energy use and a 632 MW peak. When more than one year is optimized, each later year multiplies the whole hourly profile by its annual energy relative to 2023, so energy and peak grow at the same rate: NamPower's reported energy for years up to 2025 (`demand.csv`), then the 2025 value (4,322 GWh) grown at 2.7% per year, the average 2021–2040 growth in the draft NIRP 2022 load forecast (as used in the TMP 2023 study, p. 32: 4,989 GWh in 2030, 6,511 GWh in 2040). This gives 4,938 GWh in 2030 and 6,445 GWh in 2040; the NIRP's peak demand grows slightly faster (about 2.8% per year). Large new loads such as green hydrogen are not included. The NIRP report itself could not be accessed (mme.gov.na unreachable), so the rate should be checked against its Chapter 3 and Appendix A2.

The model uses the supplied 2023 roster: 347 MW hydro, 120 MW coal nameplate (96 MW available at Van Eck), 22.5 MW installed Anixas I (21.5 MW net output limit), 6.22 MW wind, and 193.339 MW solar including NamPower's 20 MW Omburu plant. The 23 rows in `renewable_plants.csv` are the active 2023 roster; project categories are retained where supplied. Solar plants outside that roster remain in `renewable_plants_future_expansion.csv` and are not included in the model. Wind and solar availability changes hourly using the supplied Renewables.ninja profiles. Profiles are normalized by their simulated capacity, scaled to listed plant capacity, and combined into technology-wide availability. The capacity factors in the roster are the means of the hourly profiles.

The model has four nodes: Namibia (`NAM`), South Africa (`ZAF`), Zambia (`ZMB`) and Zimbabwe (`ZWE`). Namibia holds all demand and plants. Electricity is bought only at the three neighbour nodes, sold only at South Africa (NamPower's exports are SAPP sales), and flows to and from Namibia over the `power_line` transport technology. Its capacity per direction is the SAPP applicable transfer limit, the lowest of the thermal, voltage and stability limits: 575 MW from South Africa (Aggeneis–Kokerboom 220 kV, 195 MW, plus Aries–Kokerboom 400 kV, 380 MW). For Zambia (Livingstone–Sesheke–Zambezi–Gerus; SAPP lists two rows for this path in series, counted once) SAPP gives 180 MW, but the model uses the 300 MW installed transmission capacity of the Zambezi Link Interconnector (ZLI, the Caprivi Link HVDC; NamPower Integrated Annual Report 2023, key statistics p. 22; `model_limit_mw` in `interconnectors.csv`). The ZLI carried 1,396 GWh in FY2022/23 (159.4 MW on average, availability 99.69%; IAR 2023 p. 84), less than the ZESCO (1,478 GWh) and ZPC (330 GWh) deliveries combined, so about 412 GWh of contract energy reached Namibia by another route, most likely wheeled through South Africa. In the 2023 run the model imports only 603 GWh via Zambia (ZESCO 460 GWh, ZPC 143 GWh) and routes correspondingly more through South Africa, because the contracts have no minimum volumes and cost more than off-peak SAPP power. Zimbabwe has no direct line to Namibia: ZPC supply reaches it through Zambia over the Kariba North–Kariba South 330 kV link (two lines, 428 MW), so it shares the Zambia–Namibia link. SAPP lists each line in one direction only, so the same limits are assumed for the reverse direction. SAPP market trade reaches Namibia through South Africa and Zambia. Angola and Botswana are not modeled: they have only small distribution-level links with Namibia (BPC supplied 0.4 GWh in 2023), and the 400 kV Angola link (Kunene–Cahama) is planned but not built. Line losses and line costs are not modeled; a negligible €0.01/MWh wheeling cost only stops the solver from routing power in loops. South Africa represents the SAPP market and Eskom: imports there cost the 2023 SAPP day-ahead market price by time of use. Hourly SAPP prices are not public (only to SAPP members or by paid subscription), so each hour gets its month's average off-peak, standard or peak price from the SAPP monthly reports, from €20.30/MWh (June off-peak) to €228.53/MWh (November peak); USc/kWh is converted with the ECB monthly USD/EUR rate. January's peak price (9.6 USc/kWh) is quoted in the February report. SAPP does not define the time-of-use hours, so Eskom's Megaflex periods in force in 2023 are assumed: on weekdays, peak 07–10 and 18–20 and standard 06–07, 10–18 and 20–22 from September to May, and peak 06–09 and 17–19 and standard 09–17 and 19–22 from June to August; on Saturdays, standard 07–12 and 18–20; all other hours, Sundays and public holidays (treated as normal days) are off-peak. With these hours the monthly means fall within 3% of SAPP's published monthly average prices (yearly mean €86.56/MWh vs €86.16/MWh). The May and June reports note evening peaks of 16 and 14.2 USc/kWh, above the monthly peak average; these are not modeled. Zambia and Zimbabwe represent the ZESCO and ZPC contracts and cost NamPower's average import price (N$1,895/MWh, €94.97/MWh), because per-contract prices are not published. Exports earn the hourly SAPP price, capped at that contract import price: ZEN-garden has no standard constraint limiting exports to Namibia's own surplus, and the cap stops the model from buying contract power and reselling it into SAPP in hours when the market price is higher. The average export price in `prices.csv` (N$859.25/MWh) is kept for comparison only. The model therefore exports only Namibia's own surplus (11.6 GWh in 2023, from Ruacana and solar at midday in the wet months). NamPower's reported exports (198 GWh in FY2022/23, 540 GWh in FY2023/24) are mostly contract imports it could not use, which it sold "on a portfolio basis" because of lower than expected demand (NamPower Integrated Annual Report 2023, p. 142); this resale, driven by minimum volumes in the ZESCO contracts that are not published, is not modeled. Imports at Zambia and Zimbabwe are capped per hour at the contract sizes in `import_contracts.csv`: ZESCO 180 MW firm (100 MW from 2020 plus 80 MW from 2022) and ZPC 80 MW (15-year PPA from 2015). Eskom's 100 MW firm plus 300 MW non-firm contract is not capped separately, because Eskom and SAPP market trade share the South Africa node, limited by the 575 MW interconnector. Contracts are dispatchable within these caps at a flat price, with no take-or-pay volumes, so they are used only when SAPP is dearer. In 2023 ZESCO actually ran at 94% of its contract and ZPC at 47%. Contract sizes come from press reports (Lusaka Times and The Brief 2023; NewsDay 2014; Republikein 2024); contract prices are not published.

Hydro availability follows a seasonal profile based on precipitation near Ruacana. The weather point does not measure river flow. The profile is calibrated to a 1,323 GWh annual upper limit from NamPower's fiscal-year 2023 report, so treat it as an assumption for calendar 2023, not measured monthly generation.

Renewable plants have fixed O&M only; their variable O&M is taken as zero. Ruacana uses the NIRP 2022 Table 5 fixed cost of N$1,020/kW-year (2020–2021 data), scaled to 2023 with CPI: N$1,213,698/MW-year, €60,827/MW-year. As a cross-check, N$1,020/kW-year is about US$62.1/kW-year at the 2020 rate of 16.43 N$/USD, close to IRENA's Africa planning value for large hydropower (>100 MW) of US$65/kW-year; both are about 3% a year of IRENA's US$2,167/kW installed cost. PV and onshore wind fixed O&M are technology costs in USD and are not scaled with Namibian CPI, because they have fallen over time (IRENA: utility PV O&M median −5% in 2023; onshore wind full-service contract prices −74% from 2010 to 2023). PV uses IRENA's *Renewable Power Generation Costs in 2023* average all-in O&M cost for utility-scale PV of US$6.2/kW-year (global survey, 2023 USD). Onshore wind uses IRENA's *Planning and Prospects for Renewable Power: Africa* (2021) value of US$68/kW-year (2020 USD, as used in the TMP (2023) least-cost study for Namibia), unscaled as a 2023 value; it lies within IRENA's 2023 country range of US$20/kW-year (Brazil) to US$100/kW-year (Japan), with Germany at US$53.1/kW-year, and the 2023 report has no African value. Both are converted at the ECB 2023 average of 1.0813 USD/EUR: €5,734/MW-year (PV) and €62,889/MW-year (wind). Fixed O&M does not change dispatch, because the fleet is fixed; it adds about €22.6M a year to total cost. Most PV and wind plants are IPPs, so NamPower actually pays them a contract price per kWh, which is not public; the IRENA O&M values are a technology benchmark, not that payment.

## Important limitations

Van Eck is modeled with 120 MW nameplate and 96 MW available capacity. Its annual generation is not capped at the 44.3 GWh reported for FY2023/24 because that is not a calendar-2023 value. The 2023 baseline uses NamPower's FY2022/23 coal price of N$2,227/t; the FY2023/24 value of N$2,246/t is retained in `prices.csv` for comparison and is not used for calendar 2023. Fuel and O&M are separate: coal consumption is represented by the 15.81 GJ/MWh heat rate (equivalent to about 0.58 t/MWh), while Van Eck variable and fixed O&M use the NIRP 2022 values, based on 2020–2021 data, of N$1.445/kWh (N$1,445/MWh) and N$1,692/kW-year (N$1,692,000/MW-year). The model scales these and the Anixas O&M values from 2020 to 2023 prices with the Bank of Namibia all-items CPI (167.3/140.6 = 1.1899; Quarterly Bulletin, March 2026, file `march 2026.indb`), giving N$1,719/MWh and N$2,013,312/MW-year for Van Eck, then converts them to euros at the 2023 average rate of 19.9534 N$/EUR, calculated from the twelve monthly period-average exchange rates published by the Bank of Namibia in its December 2024 Quarterly Bulletin, Table IV.J, page 147. At N$2,227/t, fuel is approximately N$1,292/MWh; adding 2023-price variable O&M gives approximately N$3,011/MWh before fixed O&M. Fixed O&M is a fleet-year cost and variable O&M is charged per generated MWh. These NIRP values reflect 2020–2021 data, not calendar 2023, and are not verified actual Van Eck expenditure.

Anixas I is represented separately as an HFO plant: the model retains NamPower's 22.5 MW installed capacity and caps net output at the NIRP's 21.5 MW. Its 8.03 GJ/MWh full-load heat rate is paired with an estimated 2023 delivered HFO price of about N$306.5/GJ. The NIRP 2020 delivered price (N$173.10/GJ) is split into the 2020 NW Europe HFO 380 cst FOB market price (EUR 188.89/t, INSEE series 010751333; N$87.32/GJ at 40.6 GJ/t and the Bank of Namibia 2020 average rate of 18.7695 N$/EUR, the mean of the 12 monthly averages) and a delivery cost of N$85.78/GJ. The 2023 price is the 2023 FOB price (EUR 416.05/t, N$204.47/GJ) plus the delivery cost scaled to 2023 with CPI (N$102.06/GJ). This is an estimate, not NamPower's actual 2023 procurement price. Anixas fixed O&M of N$1,517/kW-year (N$1,805,079/MW-year at 2023 prices) is included. Anixas variable O&M is the NIRP entry “3 x N$1.55/kWh”, applied as N$4.65/kWh (N$4,650/MWh; N$5,533/MWh at 2023 prices) per generated MWh; it excludes fuel. NamPower reports Anixas generation of 6.350 GWh in FY2022/23 and 5.237 GWh in FY2023/24; these fiscal-year totals are comparison points, not calendar-2023 dispatch. Combined fuel subsidies are not used as Anixas fuel costs. The hourly generation mix is a model result, not recorded plant dispatch.

## Sources

Demand, capacities, imports, exports, and Anixas generation are based on the baseline workbook, PyPSA demand data, and NamPower reports and statistics for 2019–2025. Van Eck and Anixas technical and O&M values (based on 2020–2021 data) and the 2020 delivered HFO price are from the [Namibia NIRP Review and Update (2022)](https://www.mme.gov.na/files/publications/611_NIRP_2022for_theElectricitySupplyIndustry_NamibiaSigned.pdf). SAPP market prices are from the monthly [SAPP Market Performance Reports](https://www.sapp.co.zw/market-reports) for January to December 2023, and USD/EUR rates from the ECB euro reference rates (series EXR.M.USD.EUR.SP00.A). Interconnector transfer limits are from the [SAPP Transfer Limits table](https://www.sapp.co.zw/transfer-limits) (undated). Renewable fixed O&M benchmarks are from IRENA, *Planning and Prospects for Renewable Power: Africa* (2021), as tabulated in the [TMP (2023) least-cost study for Namibia](https://www.internationalrivers.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/86/2023/07/TMP-report-Namibia-2023.pdf), Table 2. HFO market prices are from INSEE series 010751333 (heavy fuel oil NW Europe 380 cst FOB). The 2020 and 2023 N$/EUR rates (means of monthly averages) are from the Bank of Namibia (bon.com.na; 2023 from the December 2024 Quarterly Bulletin, Table IV.J, page 147). The CPI used to scale NIRP costs to 2023 is from the Bank of Namibia Quarterly Bulletin, March 2026 (file `march 2026.indb`). Anixas FY2022/23 and FY2023/24 sent-out energy is reported in NamPower's Integrated Annual Report 2024 (the earlier nampower.com.na link to the PDF no longer works). Wind and solar profiles are from Renewables.ninja under the CC BY 4.0 license; files were renamed to `pv_<site>.csv` and `wind_<site>.csv`, retaining their original attribution headers. Hydro weather is from Renewables.ninja. The source PDFs are not stored in this repository.
